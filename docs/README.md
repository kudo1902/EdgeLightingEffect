# Documentation

Everything written about this library, in four folders and five pages:

| where | what is in it | how far to trust it |
| ----- | ------------- | ------------------- |
| [`guides/`](guides/) | how the system works, to learn it | current |
| [`reference/`](reference/) | how a part works today and why: fields, parameters, mechanisms, open defects | current; where it disagrees with the headers under `lib/include/`, the headers win |
| [`plans/`](plans/) | work proposed or only partly built | read its status line first |
| [`history/`](history/) | finished plans, measurements and the progress log: how the code got here and why | true when written; each says what is current now |
| the `.html` pages here | the neon renderer explained in three depths, and two pages of pictures | current |
| [`images/`](images/) | every figure | a folder with a `diagrams.py` is drawn by it: edit the script and rerun it, never the SVGs |

## Guides

- [`guides/architecture.md`](guides/architecture.md) - the system map: components and the direction they depend in, ownership and lifetimes, the two-phase frame, how renderers share stages, buffers and one quad through the `ResourceContext` and read each other's output (registry, reads, the PUBLIC glow product), the invariants that cross components with what catches each, the extension points and the gates, with seven diagrams (SVG in `docs/images/architecture/`, drawn by `diagrams.py` there - edit the script and rerun it, not the SVGs). Start here for the whole.
- [`guides/implementation.md`](guides/implementation.md) - brief: how the library is put together on the C++ side and how a frame runs. Start here for the C++ side.
- [`guides/neon-onboarding-guide.md`](guides/neon-onboarding-guide.md) - the long on-ramp for someone new to graphics: the GPU concepts the neon relies on (pipeline, coordinate spaces, textures, std140 blocks, FBOs, premultiplied alpha, SDFs, tone mapping, derivatives), then every `NeonConfig` field traced to its uniform, pass and dirty flag, every GL object, every pass with its inputs / state / outputs, and each shader stage by stage. Ends with pitfalls, recipes and exercises. Illustrated throughout with renders from the library itself - each layer and config field on screen, and what every pass writes, captured from a real frame. Read it before the three neon documents below if the vocabulary is new.
- [`guides/neon-shared-buffer.md`](guides/neon-shared-buffer.md) - sharing the neon's picture, in plain language: the two PUBLIC parts the screen is drawn from (`NeonRenderer.Glow`, `NeonRenderer.Ring` - the ring atlas) and how a reader samples them (`neon-light.glsl`, `GetSharedLight` / `UploadSharedLight`, a declared read), and the flat shared buffer (`NeonRenderer.Composite`) assembled from them; then, in its section 4, how the neon renderer itself uses them - every member, program and mesh involved, one frame's decisions in order (`decisions.svg`: needed, reused or drawn, the shared light, the shared buffer, what pass 2c draws), what each kind of frame does, the rules that keep the parts honest with the gate that checks each, and lifetimes and failures; a code map. Its diagrams, and the plan's, are SVGs in `docs/images/neon-shared-buffer/`, drawn by `diagrams.py` there (it borrows the architecture page's helpers) - edit the script and rerun it, not the SVGs. Start here before touching passes 1t, 1s or 2c.
- [`guides/neon-shared-buffer-explained.md`](guides/neon-shared-buffer-explained.md) - the same subject for understanding rather than use: the neon's parts in one minute, where the shared buffer sits among them (`components.svg`), part by part what it takes from each and what it changes (only the ring: while it is wanted the ring goes through its atlas on moving frames too), two frame-by-frame examples, common questions and a glossary. Read it first if the shared buffer is new to you.
- [`guides/upgrade-notes.md`](guides/upgrade-notes.md) - what a host moving from `main` has to change: the opaque fill's own cutoffs (default off - a fill that leaned on the glow's cutoffs grows to the viewport), `Cutoff::softness` running outward from `size`, the deprecated C shims for the old opaque softness, and the per-path shader compile. Read before telling anyone an upgrade is drop-in.

## Reference

- [`reference/config-reference.md`](reference/config-reference.md) - every `Config` field: its type, default, range and clamp, what it does, and which buffers each renderer registers and when they are current. The table to check a field's exact semantics against; `lib/include/core/config.h` is the source of truth.
- [`reference/emission-prepass.md`](reference/emission-prepass.md) - the neon's emission pre-pass (pass 0): why the per-sample emission is baked into a table the gather reads, the purity invariant (a pure function of `(si, uTime, config)` goes in the pre-pass; anything reading `vPos` stays in the shading), the skip on frames none of its inputs moved (section 3: what its key is and how to add an input), and the measured before/after (section 7).
- [`reference/effect-reference.md`](reference/effect-reference.md) - per-parameter reference and recipes.
- [`reference/spotlight-renderer.md`](reference/spotlight-renderer.md) - the same for the spotlight layer: every `SpotLight` field, the app-coordinate convention, the animatable surface, and the measured cost of its resolution scale.
- [`reference/glow-inner-reach.md`](reference/glow-inner-reach.md) - the glow quad's interior hole with no cutoff set: why the interior was shaded to the centre, why the hole cannot sit at `reach` (the halo and the corner arcs' bloom are still lit past it), the closed-form bound that places it, the byte-for-byte proof it changes no pixel, and before/after frame times. Read before touching the halo / bloom terms in `neon.frag`, which `GetGlowInnerReach` mirrors.
- [`reference/neon-frag-notes.md`](reference/neon-frag-notes.md) - the measured history behind `neon.frag`'s lines, moved verbatim out of the shader in that plan's step 6 (one section per block, in shader order): the sweeps, before/after numbers and bug narratives its comments used to carry inline. The shader keeps the invariant, the CPU mirror and the warning at each line; read the matching section here before changing a block whose reason the shader only summarises.
- [`reference/corner-crease-and-filament-nyquist.md`](reference/corner-crease-and-filament-nyquist.md) - the analytic emission's measured defects and their fixes: the dark diagonal wedges at the corners (halo and bloom were the field of ONE infinite edge, now a sum over the emitter's pieces), the `resolutionScale` 0.5 mismatch at thin line widths (the filament's floor was in the wrong units, and then - section 2.8 - was a fixed half width when what decides the blit is the profile's SHAPE, so a soft `filamentFalloff` rendered twice as wide), and the corner over-extension the first fix introduced (the straights ran to the SHARP corner, so a phantom emitter lit the outside of every rounded corner; they now stop at the tangent points and each arc is developed onto its own tangent), and the crease THAT fix introduced (the arc was developed at rate `r`, which is right only for a fragment on the arc, so every arc's centre of curvature carried a C1 kink and an under-count - a dark cross at the middle of a circle; section 1.9). Section 1.10 is the one level up: all of that fixed the halo/bloom FIELD, while the coverage that SCALES it was still read at the fragment's NEAREST perimeter point - so any partly lit perimeter (a half-ring arc, a segment boost) cut the glow to a hard-edged polygon along the medial axis until the glow took a gathered coverage instead (itself replaced since V20 by each piece's own coverage, read from a baked table). Includes the per-edge bloom pedestal the first fix forced and the one shared pedestal the arcs are allowed instead, the small-rect and INTERIOR brightness changes the segment sum causes - section 1.5.2 is the one to read if someone reports "the glow got bigger" - and the offscreen probes behind every number. Read before touching the halo/bloom or filament blocks.
- [`reference/glow-side-comparison.md`](reference/glow-side-comparison.md) - what changed when every edge the neon draws became COVERAGE applied to the graded output rather than a multiply into the linear emission, and when the one-sided cut moved off the reduced-resolution buffer into `neon-blit.frag`. Magnified before/after crops plus the sub-pixel sweeps behind them. Read it before retuning `glowSideSoftness` or `Cutoff::softness`, and before assuming a mask belongs above the tone map.
- [`reference/review-findings.md`](reference/review-findings.md) - open defects and rough edges, visual ones with offscreen repros. Check here before assuming a behaviour is intended.
- [`reference/naming-review.md`](reference/naming-review.md) - identifier audit against `AGENTS.md`, plus the names that describe mechanisms the code no longer has. Read before renaming anything.

## Plans

- [`plans/neon-perf-plan.md`](plans/neon-perf-plan.md) - what is left after the 1.0 gather split, measured on an Apple M2 Pro at `0cec9f4`: where a neon frame's time and memory go by frame type (still, hue rotating, intensity pulse, arc wipe, travelling segment), the attribution of the partly lit premium, and a ranked plan of thirteen items with prototype numbers for the two largest - skipping the offscreen passes on frames where nothing moved (byte-identical, 2-6x on still frames at 0.5) and re-baking only the glow coverage table's changed pieces (exact, up to 1.91x on travelling-segment frames). Its section 11 is the follow-up review and what it built: `perimeterPosition` skipped on a uniform ring (I45) and the hue-invariant field at 1.0 (I46). Read before starting any neon perf or memory work.
- [`plans/neon-reduced-scale-plan.md`](plans/neon-reduced-scale-plan.md) - the owner's target from 2026-10-07 (frame rate with `resolutionScale` below 1.0), measured at `b0ac8cf` on an Apple M2 Pro: what a frame costs by frame type and by pass below 1.0 (config-animated frames are 4-10x a still or hue frame, and below ~0.35 the passes the scale does not shrink - gather, coverage bake, edge ring, blit - are most of them), why `neon-scale-check time` overstates moving frames' CPU (it never swaps, so the driver submits mid-frame), the cleanups that move no pixel, and a ranked plan starting with what must be measured on the target. Section 9 is what is left after I53-I57, re-measured: the lens flare's own scale, arc-wipe and segment frames, the blit. Read before any work on the neon's speed below 1.0.
- [`plans/neon-shared-parts-plan.md`](plans/neon-shared-parts-plan.md) - PROPOSED 2026-10-18, after the owner's decision that readers can run our GLSL: make the two parts (`NeonRenderer.Glow`, `NeonRenderer.Ring`) the product - a `NeonLight` description found through the effect or the context, versioned, one declaration, and `neonLightOverSquare` so a reader builds a flat texture in its own pass - and remove the flat shared buffer (`NeonRenderer.Composite`, `compositeScale`) from the neon. Written as a handoff brief another agent can execute: ground rules, a baseline step 0, then six steps each with files, symbols, changes, pitfalls, checks and a commit, and an appendix of what step 3 removes; the fill is the owner's open question before step 3. Read before touching the shared products.
- [`plans/resource-sharing-plan.md`](plans/resource-sharing-plan.md) - a plan from 2026-10-15, of which S0 (`tools/layer-check`), S1 (the shader cache), S2 (registry v2: owned, keyed, stamped buffer records), S3 (the two-phase frame), S4 (read declarations), S5 (one shared fullscreen quad; the debug strip reading the neon's live ring) and S6 (one copy of the glow's edge mask; the glow as a PUBLIC product - its pilot reader, droplets lit by the glow, was built, measured and dropped) are built, with their measurements in its section 7: turning the `BufferRegistry` into a `ResourceContext` so renderers can share textures, FBOs, shader objects and temporary targets, with one owner writing each and others only reading. Covers what each renderer owns and what is duplicated, the owned and stamped registry, the two-phase frame (`Prepare` / `Render`), public products versus internal intermediates (the neon's reduced buffer is not its picture), and why pooling saves nothing across today's layers. Steps S0-S7, starting with a shared shader-object cache. The owner's decisions (2026-10-15) are in its section 5. Read before any work on sharing resources between renderers.

## History

- [`history/neon-unification-plan.md`](history/neon-unification-plan.md) - how the half-res neon fork was folded into `NeonRenderer` as a resolution scale, and the debug overlays split into `DebugRenderer`; and how both fork merges (the neon's and the lens flare's, the last fork in the tree) were verified: eleven scenes, nine byte-identical and two differing only in the bounding box's compositing order; twelve flare scenes, all byte-identical, plus the two defects that merge closed. Read it if a doc or comment still refers to `NeonOptimizedRenderer`.
- [`history/lens-flare-perf-review.md`](history/lens-flare-perf-review.md) - why the flare was the pipeline's most expensive layer and what the five changes that halved it did. Per-term cost attribution, the tuning header those changes forced, and the reason one of them cannot be byte-identical on any GPU. Read before touching `lens-flare.frag`'s ghost loop.
- [`history/neon-perf-review.md`](history/neon-perf-review.md) - the same for the neon layer, which is 81% of the frame and whose gather loop is 95% of that. Per-term attribution, the three changes that took it to 1.46x, and the two directions that were measured and set aside (a windowed gather; an interior hole in the draw quad - since built without the inner fade it called for, as `GetGlowInnerReach`; see `docs/reference/glow-inner-reach.md`). Section 8 is the measured shape of `resolutionScale` / `numSamples` / `glowRadius` - read it before tuning any of them for speed. Section 10 is the later round that took scale 1.0 another 2.3-4.4x at 1080p (the gather split out at 1.0, the single-`atan` bloom, and the exact / bounded piece skips), with the attribution of the shading pass that is now the cost. Read before touching `neon.frag`'s gather loop or its halo / bloom pieces.
- [`history/neon-shader-cleanup-plan.md`](history/neon-shader-cleanup-plan.md) - the neon's program slots, `neon.frag` variants and hand-copied shader functions as of `1488648`, and a stepwise plan to cut them to what earns its place without moving a pixel: one copy of the SDF / tone-map / edge-mask code, one predicate for a uniformly lit ring, the never-built inline-gather path removed (an owner's decision), and the measured reasons behind each. Every step is done (I49); its section 1 is the state BEFORE the cleanup, so it is the record of why each shortcut was kept or cut - the live program list is the `ShaderProgram` members in `lib/include/renderer/neon-renderer.h`. Read it before re-adding a variant or program it removed.
- [`history/neon-animation-perf-analysis.md`](history/neon-animation-perf-analysis.md) - measured on an AMD Radeon Pro 5300M on 2026-10-08, after a report that the frame rate dropped while several arcs or segments changed length: what each frame type costs pass by pass (still 0.42 ms, rotating hue 0.9, 8 arcs moving 3.1, 8 segments 5.4 at 0.5), fragments and visible share per pass, where the time goes inside the shading (the analytic pieces are over half of it), the two exact fixes built that day (`filamentLit`, the per-type coverage re-bake), and a ranked list of what is left - a lower scale, a two-channel field for segment configs, approximations in the bake - plus what was ruled out. Read before any neon perf work on animated frames.
- [`history/neon-glow-cover-resolution-plan.md`](history/neon-glow-cover-resolution-plan.md) - why the glow coverage bake was the largest pass of every frame that moved a segment, and the plan that sized the table per light type: the layout as a parameter stored with the table (I59), the arcs' width from the rect and the halo width (I59), the segments' own narrow table and its fill pass (I60), the narrower bell support (I61) and pass 1b without the filament (I62). Section 11 is what was built and measured on an AMD Radeon Pro 5300M on 2026-10-09 (the band's eight-segment frame 1.05 -> 0.61 ms), including the 8-node rule that failed its criterion and the finding that this GPU's `GL_TIME_ELAPSED` reads ~3.85x the wall-clock time - every timer figure before it is a ratio. Read before touching the coverage bake, the fill or the table's width.
- [`history/spotlight-renderer-plan.md`](history/spotlight-renderer-plan.md) - the `SpotlightRenderer` design and the offscreen verification behind it, including the solved strip bound and the one real defect that verification caught.
- [`history/neon-resolution-scale-plan.md`](history/neon-resolution-scale-plan.md) and [`neon-resolution-scale-comparison.html`](neon-resolution-scale-comparison.html) - the edge ring: why the reduced-resolution neon re-shades a thin ring around the edge at full resolution (the plan's section 0, the proposal condensed), how it was built in six steps and what each measured (the rest of the plan; section 7 has the re-verification and the cost caveats, section 11 the open decisions), and twelve scenes at six scales against their 1.0 renders, before and after (the comparison page). [`history/neon-resolution-scale-perf-comparison.md`](history/neon-resolution-scale-perf-comparison.md) is the price: frame time before and after at every scale, attributed step by step (the ring is three quarters of it), plus startup and memory - and, in section 9, the bottom line against `main` at 0.5 on an Apple M2 Pro (2.3x faster at 720p, 2.8x at 1080p, and within 2/255 of 1.0 where `main` is up to 77 off). Section 10 is what the glow-coverage table (V19/V20) added on top, which sections 1-9 predate. Read the plan before touching the reduced-scale passes, the ring's width rule or the blit/ring partition.
- [`history/neon-shared-layer-perf-review.md`](history/neon-shared-layer-perf-review.md) - what sharing costs, measured on an Apple M2 Pro after the plan was built: nothing for a host that reads nothing, 0.016-0.033 ms a moving frame for a reader of the parts, 0.04-0.19 ms for the shared buffer - nearly all of it render passes (the atlas's and the buffer's), not shading, which a clear-only experiment shows. Per pass, the first frames, memory, against the re-shaded buffer it replaced (cheaper at 1, 1.10-1.16x dearer at 0.5: the atlas's render pass), the three changes the review made, and what is left. Read before any work on the cost of passes 1t, 1s or 2c.
- [`history/progress-log.md`](history/progress-log.md) - dated entries, newest first: what changed (with the commits), what was measured, what was decided and what is open. Start here to see where the work stands.
- [`history/neon-resolution-scale-perf-comparison.md`](history/neon-resolution-scale-perf-comparison.md) - what the edge ring cost: frame time before and after at every scale, attributed step by step, plus startup and memory; section 9 is the bottom line against `main` at 0.5 on an Apple M2 Pro, section 10 what the glow-coverage table added since.

## The pages

- [`neon-shader-outputs.html`](neon-shader-outputs.html) - one frame at resolution scale 0.5 (the default), pass by pass: what the neon's programs (`mEmissionShader`, `mGlowCoverShader`, `mGatherShader`, `mShadeShader`, the blit, `mRingShader`, the fill, the field bake `mFieldBakeShader` and its composite `mFieldCompositeShader` - under a rotating hue or a moving intensity - and the edge ring's own field, the same bake packed into four strips and its composite `mRingFieldCompositeShader`) actually wrote, at the resolution it runs, and why each is its own program - the field bake's case at length.
