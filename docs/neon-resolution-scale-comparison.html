When the rect moves

A still frame hides one artefact. The reduced buffer's texel grid is fixed to the viewport, so as the rect slides the filament falls at different points between texels. Here the hairline scene's rect moves down 1/8 px per step. The dashed line is the true edge; at 1.0 the line stays centred on it, at low scales it drifts off and snaps back.

How this was measured

Capture

A standalone harness linked against libedge-lighting.a at commit 51c4bb0 (branch add_separate_cutoff_for_opaque). Hidden GLFW window for a GL 3.3 core context, the neon layer alone, drawn through OffscreenCapture at an explicit 1280 x 720 over a clear of rgb(5, 5, 8). Rect 640 x 360 at (320, 180) unless the scene says otherwise. hueRotationRate and colorTransitionDuration are 0 and the clock is ticked once, so every render is deterministic.

Metrics

Per-pixel error is the largest of the three channel differences against the 1.0 render. Means and percentiles count only visibly lit pixels, meaning the brightest channel is more than 16 levels above the clear in either image, so the far bloom tail does not dilute them. PSNR is over the whole frame. "Light vs 1.0" is total luma above the clear, as a ratio.

Grades

From p99 error: match 4 or less, minor 12 or less, visible 32 or less, severe above. The lowest-scale column is the lowest scale graded minor or better. The heatmap maps error 0 to 64+ levels onto its ramp, with differences of 1 level drawn black.

Timing and motion

GPU time is one machine (), so read ratios, not milliseconds. Each scale gets a freshly initialised effect. The motion sweep moves geometry.position.y by 1/8 px for 64 steps and takes the luma-weighted centroid within 12 px of the true edge on the top edge's centre column.

Scratch harness, not checked in. Scenes and numbers regenerate from one run; nothing here is hand-edited.
